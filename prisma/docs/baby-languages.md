# Languages

She is four months old. Nothing here is a schedule — she is followed, not
scheduled — and nothing here is a backlog either. This is what I want, written
down once so it stops being re-decided.

## Vietnamese and English

Both, every day, out loud, from books.

That's it, and it is deliberately **not** tracked. It is already happening; a
tick box next to it would only ever be an audit of the thing least at risk of
being skipped. What it did produce goes in the journal — the week she started
turning toward the Vietnamese one, the first word she reacted to.

## Russian is an open problem

Her dad is Russian. He won't teach her. That is the constraint, and no amount of
planning changes it — so the language has to come from somewhere that isn't him.

Three leads, in the order they're worth trying:

1. **A Russian-speaking sitter or playgroup.** A few hours a week of a real speaker beats any amount of screen time. Highest effort, and the only one that could actually work on its own.
2. **His family, on video call, regularly.** Costs nothing and needs no buy-in from him. Wants to be a standing slot rather than an ask each time, or it quietly stops happening.
3. **A Russian shelf** — songs, cartoons, board books. Lowest effort. Won't make her fluent; will make the sound of it ordinary rather than foreign, which is most of what the first years are for.

## Chinese is a newer one

Added 2026-08-05, and unlike Russian there is no constraint to work around and
no leads yet — which is the whole reason it is a task on this area rather than a
paragraph here. The three Russian leads are probably the right shape to copy,
but that is a guess until one of them is actually tried.

## What is not here

There is no cadence, no drift warning and nothing to fall behind on. That was
tried — a "Multilingual baby" project with a two-day cadence — and it was the
wrong instrument: a project reports on something that would not otherwise
happen, and this happens anyway. What replaced it is this page for what I want,
the journal for what she actually did, and the odd real task for the parts that
genuinely need figuring out.
