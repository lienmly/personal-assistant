# Three languages, one of them unsolved

She is four months old. Nothing here is a schedule — she is followed, not
scheduled — but three things are deliberate.

## Vietnamese and English

Both, every day, out loud, from books. That's it. It is two recurring tasks
rather than a calendar block because it is something **owed**, not something
that happens at a fixed time — it gets done in whatever gap the day leaves.

Ticking one records the day and moves it to tomorrow. Missing a day is missing
a day; it does not come back as a backlog.

## Russian is the open problem

Her dad is Russian. He won't teach her. That is the constraint, and no amount
of planning changes it — so the language has to come from somewhere that isn't
him.

Three leads, in the order they're worth trying:

1. **A Russian-speaking sitter or playgroup.** A few hours a week of a real
   speaker beats any amount of screen time. Highest effort, and the only one
   that could actually work on its own.
2. **His family, on video call, regularly.** Costs nothing and needs no buy-in
   from him. Wants to be a standing slot rather than an ask each time, or it
   quietly stops happening.
3. **A Russian shelf** — songs, cartoons, board books. Lowest effort. Won't
   make her fluent; will make the sound of it ordinary rather than foreign,
   which is most of what the first years are for.

## Why this is a project and not a list

Because it feeds the content. "Raising her in two languages when only one of
them is easy" and "her dad is Russian and won't teach her" are both real Coding
Mom posts, and this is where the footage and the ending come from. Those items
carry the Coding Mom brand and *this* project — the two axes, so posting one
counts as this project moving.
